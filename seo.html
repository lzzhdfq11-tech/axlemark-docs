SEO and GEO

Axlemark outputs clean titles, canonical URLs, social sharing tags and schema.org structured data from your own store data, and gives you content sections (FAQ, buying guides, brand directory, specifications tables) that search engines and AI assistants can read and cite. This page explains what is output, which settings control it, and how to be understood by search engines and generative AI.

Titles and meta descriptions

  • Page title: the SEO title you set on each product, collection, page or article (Search engine listing in your admin), followed by your store name when the title does not already contain it. Tag-filtered pages add the tags and paginated pages add “Page 2”, so every URL has a unique title.
  • Meta description: the SEO description from the same place. For the home page, set it in Online Store → Preferences.

Canonical URLs and indexing

  • Every page has a canonical link from Shopify, so product URLs inside collections point to the main product URL.
  • noindex, follow is added to pages that should not appear in search results: search results, the 404 page, the cart, collections with a sort order in the URL and empty collections.
  • Gift card pages are never indexed.

Social sharing tags

Each page outputs og:title, og:description, og:url, og:type, og:locale and a 1200px image with alt text, plus matching twitter: meta tags for social cards.

Structured data (JSON-LD)

Structured data is machine-readable information, in the schema.org vocabulary, that tells search engines and AI assistants exactly what a page is about: your store, a product with its price and availability, a guide, or a set of questions and answers. Axlemark outputs it as JSON-LD, using only your own store data:

TypeWhereBuilt from
OnlineStore, Store, AutoPartsStore, ElectronicsStore or OrganizationEvery pageStore name and URL, logo from Settings → Brand, store description, social links, customer service phone and email, opening hours (store types with a physical location) and the city, region and country from Settings → Store details.
WebSite with SearchActionEvery pageStore name, language and your search URL, so search engines can offer a search box for your store.
BreadcrumbListProduct, collection, blog, article and pageHome → collection → product, or Home → blog → article.
Product / ProductGroupProduct pagesGenerated by Shopify from the product: title, description, images, variants, SKU, price, availability, brand (vendor). Products with variants are output as a ProductGroup.
ArticleBlog postsGenerated by Shopify from the article.
FAQPagePages with an FAQ sectionThe FAQ section’s questions and answers, when Add FAQ structured data is on.

Example: what the store and website data looks like

This is a shortened example of the JSON-LD printed on every page (values come from your store; empty fields are left out):

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    {
      "@type": "AutoPartsStore",
      "@id": "https://your-store.example/#store",
      "name": "Your store",
      "url": "https://your-store.example/",
      "logo": "https://cdn.example/logo.png",
      "sameAs": ["https://social.example/yourstore"],
      "contactPoint": { "@type": "ContactPoint", "contactType": "customer service", "telephone": "+1 555 0100" },
      "openingHours": "Mo-Fr 09:00-17:00"
    },
    {
      "@type": "WebSite",
      "@id": "https://your-store.example/#website",
      "name": "Your store",
      "inLanguage": "en",
      "publisher": { "@id": "https://your-store.example/#store" },
      "potentialAction": {
        "@type": "SearchAction",
        "target": { "@type": "EntryPoint", "urlTemplate": "https://your-store.example/search?q={search_term_string}" },
        "query-input": "required name=search_term_string"
      }
    }
  ]
}

The store and the website are linked with @id, so crawlers understand that the website is published by your store. Product pages add Shopify’s own Product or ProductGroup data (with offers, SKU, brand and availability) and a BreadcrumbList.

  • Business type: choose Auto parts store or Electronics store when you have a physical location of that kind, Store with a physical location for other shops, and Online store (default) when you sell online only.
  • Customer service hours: one range per line in the format Mo-Fr 09:00-17:00. Hours are added to structured data only for the store types with a physical location. They also show in the footer Contact details block.

Turning structured data off

If an SEO or reviews app already adds store and product structured data, two copies can confuse search engines. Uncheck Theme settings → Store details → Add structured data. This removes the store, website, breadcrumb, product and article JSON-LD.

FAQ structured data is controlled separately in each FAQ section with Add FAQ structured data. Keep it on in only one FAQ section per page, and turn it off if an app adds FAQ data for the same page.

Check your markup

After changes, test a product page, a collection and the home page in a rich results or schema validation tool and fix any warnings about missing product data.

What GEO means for a parts store

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of making your store easy for AI assistants and AI search features, such as chat assistants, answer engines, AI overviews in search results and Shopify’s own AI tools, to understand correctly, quote and recommend. These systems read the same pages, structured data and product data as search engines, then summarize them into an answer. For a parts store the questions are concrete: “Which brake pads fit a 2016 Model A1?”, “Which toner works with a P200 printer?”, “Who stocks Brand B filters?”. An assistant can only answer with your store if your pages state those facts clearly, in text, on crawlable URLs.

There is no special markup that guarantees AI visibility. What works is the same as good SEO: accurate, specific, consistent information that is easy to extract.

llms.txt and Shopify

llms.txt is a proposed plain-text file at the root of a website that gives AI tools a short, link-rich summary of the site. Shopify can serve one for your store at /llms.txt; it is generated by Shopify, not by the theme, so Axlemark does not change it. Open /llms.txt on your store in a browser to see what it contains. Since it reflects your store description, policies, collections and products, the way to improve it is to keep that admin data complete and up to date.

This documentation site has its own llms.txt describing the Axlemark theme.

Recommended practices for merchants

  1. Write specific product titles: part type, position and key size, for example “Front brake pads, ceramic, 4-piece set”, not just a part number.
  2. Complete product data: description, vendor (brand), product type and Shopify product category, SKU, barcode where you have one, weight and real availability.
  3. State compatibility in text: list compatible vehicles, devices or printers in a specifications table or collapsible tab, in addition to the finder tags.
  4. Use metafields for specifications and show them with the Specifications table block, so values are consistent across products.
  5. Answer real questions in FAQ sections (fitment, returns, shipping times, warranty), with FAQ structured data on in one FAQ section per page.
  6. Publish buying guides that explain how to choose a part, and connect them to products with the custom.related_products metafield or a collection tag.
  7. Keep a brands page using the brands page template, with featured brand blocks for your main lines.
  8. Fill in store details: store description, logo, address, phone, email, hours, business type and social links, and use the same name and details everywhere you are listed.
  9. Keep policies clear: shipping, returns and warranty pages in plain language, since assistants often quote them.
  10. Avoid duplicate structured data: if an app adds the same types, turn off the theme’s structured data or the app’s, not both.
  11. Check regularly: test pages in a rich results or schema validation tool, look at your store’s /llms.txt, and ask an AI assistant questions about your products to see how your store is described.

Performance and accessibility

Axlemark loads no JavaScript frameworks, no jQuery and no external fonts or scripts. The shared theme CSS and JavaScript on the home page come to about 35 KB with gzip compression; section styles and scripts load only where those sections are used. Images are responsive and lazy loaded below the fold, and the first large image loads with high priority. Menus, drawers, the finder and dialogs work with the keyboard, keep focus visible and respect reduced-motion preferences. Fast, accessible pages are easier for every crawler to read. To keep your store fast:

  • Upload images at the size they are shown (a hero around 2400px wide is plenty). The theme creates responsive sizes and lazy loads images below the fold.
  • Keep the first section light: one hero image rather than a video on mobile, and avoid autoplaying carousels at the top.
  • Remove apps you no longer use. Leftover app scripts are the most common cause of slow stores.
  • Use system fonts or a single font family to cut font downloads.
  • Keep the finder JSON file lean, with only the fields you need.
  • Limit the number of home page sections and products per grid to what shoppers actually use.
  • Watch the web performance report in Analytics and test key pages on a phone.
